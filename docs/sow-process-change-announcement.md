# SOW-Centric Pipeline Change — Slack Announcement

Slack-ready articulation of the bid-to-proposal process change (replaces the
earlier pitch-deck framing). The core shift: the GFE is retired as a separate
document; the SOW built in Knack is the single scope artifact from intake
through final proposal.

---

**Heads up team — we're changing how projects move from intake to final proposal.** Here's the change in one line, then the specifics.

**The big idea:** we're retiring the GFE as a standalone document. From day one, every project lives as a **SOW in Knack**, and that one scope is the single source of truth all the way through. Nothing gets re-keyed into a quote at a later stage — the SOW just gets progressively more accurate: we draft it, the survey validates it, the sub's bid prices it, Sales shapes it with the client, and the final proposal is generated from it.

What that means stage by stage:

**1. New projects drop into "Create Preliminary."**
The old Draft GFE → GFE Review → GFE Submitted stages become **Create Preliminary → Review Preliminary → SOW Submitted**. The coordinator builds the preliminary SOW in Knack right away — same second-set-of-eyes review as before, but the thing being drafted and reviewed is the scope itself, not an estimate document.

**2. The first sub bid auto-syncs into the SOW.**
When SVS drops their bid for the first time after the survey, it syncs into the SOW automatically. Nobody transcribes bid numbers into a quote — the priced scope is just there, ready for review.

**3. Sales owns "Review Bid" — and it's a client conversation now.**
Review Bid used to be a PM step (make the bid coherent, build the final quote internally). It now belongs to **Sales**, with a specific mandate: review the scope **with the client** and confirm direction — what changes, what shape the SOW should take.

**4. Sales sends back explicit SOW direction.**
Out of that conversation, Sales submits either a go-ahead or a **revision request with direction on how to shape the SOW**. Ops gets clear instructions instead of reverse-engineering intent from a thread.

**5. To Do becomes the proposal-production queue.**
Both new finals and revision requests drop into **To Do**. To Do is effectively the new "Review Bid" column for ops — the deliverable coming out of it is a **client-facing final proposal**, built from the SOW that Sales and the client have already aligned on.

**Why we're doing this:** one scope object with clear ownership at each stage — ops builds and maintains the SOW, Sales aligns it with the client, and the final proposal falls out of it. No more scope living in three documents (GFE, bid, quote) that drift apart, and no more rebuilding the quote from scratch at Review Bid.
