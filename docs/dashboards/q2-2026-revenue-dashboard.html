Colour means pacing, everywhere on this page. Red is well behind the modest goal (under 85% of it), amber is behind (85–90%), blue is near modest (90–100% — within reach, deliberately neutral rather than a warning), green clears modest, and green with a ★ gold star is stretch territory — within 10% of the stretch goal or past it. Every coloured figure also carries an icon and a word, so colour is never doing the work on its own.

How the goal maps. The $6.65M / $7.85M targets cover the whole Apr–Sep window — the “Q2 / Q3” column in the source sheet, which is why this board calls it Q2 & Q3 rather than Q2. The stretch figure is an exact match to the sheet’s own stretch scenario ($1.4M in Aug and Sep), which confirms the mapping.

Part 1 formulas (reproduced from the Daily KPIs sheet). Revenue to date = Install + eCommerce + check / money order — cheques and money orders are money already received, so they count as revenue and drive the pacing figure, not the “with pending” case. Adjusted daily average = (revenue to date − big deal) ÷ business days elapsed. Pacing to = adjusted daily average × business days in month, with the big deal added back — so a single outsized deal lands in the total without inflating the run rate. “With pending” adds pending install to revenue to date before run-rating — matching the sheet’s $1,196,356 at its own inputs, where no check / money order amount was recorded.

Business days count themselves. Days elapsed defaults to the weekdays in the current month through the end of yesterday, minus the holidays you have ticked in Backstage. Each holiday is a rule, not a fixed date, so it recomputes correctly every year; date-fixed ones shift off weekends the usual way. Untick anything SCW works, tick anything it doesn’t (MLK, Presidents’, Good Friday, Juneteenth, Indigenous Peoples’, Veterans Day ship off by default), and add shutdown days or floating holidays as one-off dates. Holidays travel with the JSON export. The ±½ buttons and direct edits are manual adjustments: enter 12.5 mid-day when 12 is the auto count. An adjustment holds only for the day it was made, then the count snaps back to auto. The days-in-month total works the same way, per month.

Period totals are derived from the monthly grid, so the table is always self-consistent with the charts. Because the monthly figures are reported rounded to the nearest $10K or so, these totals run slightly under the source sheet’s own stated totals — up to about $80K on the 2025 window. Paste exact figures into the editor above and the difference disappears.

The window’s August. With “use this month’s live pacing” switched on (the default), the Q2 & Q3 window takes its August from Part 1’s live month pacing and only September from the baseline pace. Mind the caveat below — Part 1 paces total company revenue while the window is booked revenue — so treat the blended figure as directional, and flip the toggle off for a booked-only view.

The monthly and annual figures are one measure, now reconciled. An accounting export of Total Income by month (Aug 2020 – Jul 2026) confirmed that the monthly sheet figures and the annual company totals are the same measure — the sheet was simply displaying them rounded. Every monthly figure from 2022 onward, and every annual total from 2021 to 2025, has been replaced with the exact amount. Two annual figures were materially overstated and are now corrected: 2024 from $11.87M to $11.33M (−$545K) and 2025 from $13.50M to $12.75M (−$748K). 2016–2020 predate the export and stand as previously held. August 2026 is still the booked entry, not an accounting figure.
